The launch film

Every day has a pulse.

Pulse OS brings your whole day into one place — your plans, the weather, the news, the markets, your music and your travel — on one calm screen, with an assistant that can read all of it and act on it.

The ad

Thirty seconds, made for phones.

No narration. Every part of Pulse as one clear moment — Home, the Launchpad, every calendar in one, an assistant you can talk to, the world live, your Spotify built in, and wherever you’re headed — cut to “Rising Forest” by Diego Nava.

How it was made

Drawn, not recorded.

Every frame is code

Both films are built in Remotion from Pulse’s own views, rebuilt from its components rather than recorded off a screen — so every frame is sharp at any size, and every number on screen is the one the story needs.

Cut to the word

Two voices — a narrator, and Pulse itself — generated with Google’s Gemini, each line read three times and the best take chosen blind. whisper.cpp timed every word, and the picture moves on the words, on a ninety-beat-a-minute grid with a score composed in code.

Cut to the bar

The ad has no voice at all. It runs at 124 beats a minute, edited on the bars of “Rising Forest” by Diego Nava, with forty-five sound effects set on the beats.

Sound and voice

The launch film’s score was composed for it in code. The ad’s music is “Rising Forest” by Diego Nava, and its sound effects are from Mixkit, under the Mixkit free licences.

The narration is AI-generated with Google Gemini (voices Charon and Puck) and carries Google’s SynthID watermark.
