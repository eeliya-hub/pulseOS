01 What’s new · 17 October

Your mail, your plans, your days, finally talking to each other.

Mail and Projects have tabs of their own, and the calendar has been rebuilt around the month. Two minutes on all of it, narrated.

Mail

Gmail and Outlook, read ahead. A date goes to your calendar, a line onto a board, and the reply you describe is written for you to edit.

Projects

Boards, a timeline and a table over one set of cards — and an assistant that knows what is left on them.

Schedule

The whole month at a glance, a day that says what is on now, and to‑dos that come back — every week, or until they are done.

02 Every part of it

Open the one you came for.

Nine views, in the order the app keeps them. Pick one and it is on screen.

Home

Who you are, what is next, what the sky is doing, and the handful of apps you actually use. The background is the hour itself — dawn, day, dusk and night each light the whole screen differently, so a glance tells you where you are in the day before you have read a word.

OpenWeatherGoogle CalendariCloud

Apps

Every application on your machine and every site you keep going back to, on one shelf, in folders you arrange yourself. Pulse finds what you have installed, takes any icon you like, and notices new apps while it is open.

Schedule Updated

Google Calendar, iCloud and any .ics feed in one place — read and written both ways, so an event made here is an event on your phone. The month fills the screen with each day’s events written into it, and the days either side stay quietly in view. Beside it the day: what is over steps back, what is on now is lit, and a to‑do can come back daily, weekly, monthly or yearly — or every day until it is done.

Projects New

Every plan on a board: lists you name, cards you drag between them, and a last list that ticks a card off when it lands. A card carries labels, dates, a checklist, notes and a thread of notes to self. Step back and the same cards sit on a timeline, or all in one table. A line from an email can land here as a card, and Ask Pulse can read a board and work on it.

Ask Pulse

An assistant that can see everything on this page and change it. Put the call in the diary, ask what is left on a project and move the card that is done, start the playlist, draft the reply. Type it, or hold a key and say it out loud — voice mode listens, works while you watch, and answers in kind.

GeminiOpenAIClaudeGemini Live voice

Mail New

Connect Gmail or Outlook and your email joins the rest of your day. Pulse reads ahead, so the inbox already says what needs a reply and what is due, and one press finds the meetings, the deadlines and the things you have been asked to do — each proposed for your calendar, your to‑do list or one of your projects. Highlight any line to do the same with just that. It never sends, files or deletes anything on its own.

News & Markets

A ticker running along the horizon, your own watchlist beneath it, live news channels playing in the corner, and headlines from your street as readily as from the other side of the world. Football, NBA, NFL and Formula 1 keep their own fixtures, tables and results.

Travel

A trip with the flight tracked live from community ADS‑B receivers, the local time and weather where you are going, the exchange rate with a month of history behind it, a packing list, and a map you can plan the days on. All of it works with no paid key.

03 What it plugs into

It connects to the things you already use.

Sign in once with your own account — by OAuth, never a password — and Pulse reads and writes to them directly. Nothing goes through a service of ours, because there isn’t one.

GmailOutlookGoogle CalendariCloud CalendarSpotifyGemini
And the free, keyless sources behind the rest

Plus the keyless and free-tier sources behind the rest: OpenWeather, Finnhub and CoinGecko, GNews and RSS, Football‑Data, balldontlie and Jolpica, LRCLIB lyrics, OpenStreetMap and OpenFreeMap, community ADS‑B for live flights, and the ECB's own exchange rates. OpenAI and Claude can stand in for Gemini.
